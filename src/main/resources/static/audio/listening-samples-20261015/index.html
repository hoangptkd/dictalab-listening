Trích đoạn Part 4. Hoàn thành 6 ghi chú, viết ONE WORD ONLY cho mỗi câu. Nghiên cứu trong bài là tình huống giả định phục vụ luyện nghe. Nhãn 6.0 chưa được hiệu chuẩn bằng đề thi hoặc người học.

Đáp án và giải thích
  1. observations — Bổ sung quan sát để khắc phục giới hạn trí nhớ của người trả lời.
    “observations of movement” · Đoạn 5
  2. weekend — Hai loại ngày khác nhau giúp so sánh bối cảnh.
    “one weekday and one weekend” · Đoạn 6
  3. volunteers — Người tự nguyện có thể quan tâm tới bảo tàng hơn nhóm còn lại.
    “participants are volunteers” · Đoạn 9
  4. satisfaction — Ở lâu có thể vì chỉ dẫn khó hiểu, không phải vì hài lòng.
    “visitor's satisfaction” · Đoạn 11
  5. signs — Guides là phương án bị thay thế trong thử nghiệm.
    “improve the signs” · Đoạn 13
  6. exhibition — Triển lãm có thể làm thay đổi nhóm khách và gây nhiễu kết quả.
    “A temporary exhibition” · Đoạn 17
Transcript và nghe từng đoạn
  1. Today we will examine how a museum can evaluate a new visitor route.
  2. Consider a hypothetical study in which a museum wants visitors to explore its smaller galleries.
  3. The initial proposal is to collect questionnaires at the exit, but this approach has a limitation.
  4. Visitors may remember their favourite exhibit without accurately recalling the route they followed.
  5. The team therefore combines the questionnaires with observations of movement through the building.
  6. Before introducing any changes, researchers record the existing patterns during one weekday and one weekend.
  7. This provides a baseline, although differences in attendance must be considered when the results are compared.
  8. A second issue concerns the people who agree to complete the questionnaires.
  9. If all the participants are volunteers, they may be more interested in the museum than other visitors.
  10. Consequently, their responses should not automatically be treated as representative of everyone who entered.
  11. Researchers also need to distinguish between the length of a visit and the visitor's satisfaction.
  12. Someone might remain in a gallery because the directions are confusing, rather than because the display is engaging.
  13. For the pilot, the team chooses to improve the signs instead of employing additional guides.
  14. The signs are cheaper to change, and their effect can be examined before a larger investment is made.
  15. Researchers then repeat the observations, keeping the opening hours and ticket prices unchanged.
  16. Nevertheless, an increase in visits to the smaller galleries would not by itself prove that the signs caused it.
  17. A temporary exhibition, for instance, might attract a different group of visitors during the second observation period.
  18. The final report should therefore acknowledge these uncertainties and recommend further testing, rather than promise a definite improvement.
